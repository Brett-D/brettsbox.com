My calibrated PLA profile

Klipper makes calibration measurable rather than a matter of feel. These are the numbers my PLA settled on after a round of calibration prints:

Nozzle temperature210 °CStrong layer bonding without stringing

Roomba development platform

The parts that carry the Roomba robot's brains aren't drawn by hand. They're generated by a Python script using manifold3d and trimesh. Change a dimension and the whole platform regenerates, then checks itself:

  • Automatic collision checks: the script verifies that no part intersects another, or the Roomba underneath.
  • An open frame carries the camera up front on posts, with a cradle for the Jetson and one for the battery.
  • Tool-less assembly with snap pins and dovetails, so it comes apart in seconds on the bench.
  • Keeps the essentials reachable: the CLEAN button and the serial port stay uncovered.
  • Every part fits a 229 × 208 mm bed, so the frame splits in two halves that join on dovetails.

The STLs and the generator live in roomba-jetson-stack/hardware/platform.

Stiffened BLTouch side mount

The popular side mount for the BLTouch flexed just enough to make first layers inconsistent. My remix adds material where it bends, so the probe triggers at the same height every time.

NAS enclosure

The case for my homelab server, generated by the same kind of script as the Roomba platform: the drive cage slides into the bottom and the ThinkCentre sits on top. Outside, it measures 187 × 208 × 263 mm (width × height × depth).

  • Two parts. A sleeve, and a rear cap that slides over a tongue joint and is held by four screws.
  • Made to print. The hexagonal vents point upwards so they print without supports, and the inside of the joint is chamfered at 45° so the sleeve prints standing on its open front.
  • Airflow. Vents in the sides, the front and the floor under the drives, a 50 mm gap behind the cage's fan, and a hex grille in the rear cap.
  • Room for cables. The ThinkCentre rests on two ledges over a 35 mm gap for the SATA cables. The rear cut-out for its ports is open at the top, so cables drop in from above, and there's a hole for a DC power jack.
  • Checked in code. Before exporting, the script checks that neither part hits the drive cage, the ThinkCentre, the cable space or the other part.

How I design parts

  1. Measure the real thing with calipers, twice.
  2. Describe it in code: dimensions become variables, so the design can change without starting over.
  3. Check it automatically: collisions, clearances and whether it fits the bed.
  4. Slice in OrcaSlicer with the calibrated profile.
  5. Print, test-fit, adjust a number, repeat.
