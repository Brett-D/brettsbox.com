Project 01 · Active

Roomba 550 → autonomous mapping robot

A 2007-era Roomba 550 with a Jetson Orin Nano and a stereo depth camera on its back. It maps the house with visual SLAM, plans its own routes and explores rooms it has never seen.

The goal

Old robot vacuums find walls by hitting them. I wanted to see how far a nearly twenty-year-old Roomba could go with modern robotics bolted on top: a robot that maps the house on its own, avoids obstacles it can see, and eventually becomes a smarter vacuum that cleans the rooms I pick and knows when it's finished.

The Roomba 550 is a great base for this because iRobot published its Open Interface: a serial protocol that lets you drive the wheels and read every sensor. Under the dust bin, it's a perfectly good robot.

Hardware

Base
iRobot Roomba 550 (R3 board), Open Interface at 115200 baud
Compute
NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super 8 GB, 256 GB NVMe
Vision
MYNT EYE D1000 stereo camera with IR depth and IMU, on USB 3
Serial link
FT232 USB-TTL adapter, jumper on 5 V, to the 7-pin Mini-DIN port
Power
Ridgid 18 V Li-ion pack → 7.5 A fuse → 15 V / 12 A buck regulator
Chassis
Parametric 3D-printed platform with snap pins and dovetails
Software
ROS 2 Jazzy, cuVSLAM / Basalt, robot_localization, RTAB-Map, Nav2, nvblox
Interface
Web dashboard: live map, camera, drive pad, tap-to-go, Explore & map

How it works

Navigation is a relay race from the camera to the wheels. Each stage adds something and hands on to the next, with wheel odometry and the gyro filling in whenever vision loses track:

Power and data take a simpler route: one tool battery, one regulator, one USB camera and one serial adapter.

The platform

Everything rides on a 3D-printed platform generated from Python code, with automatic collision checks, tool-less snap pins and dovetails. It keeps the CLEAN button and the serial port reachable and prints on a 229 × 208 mm bed. More on the 3D printing page.
