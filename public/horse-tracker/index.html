Horse Tracker 9000

A camera on a servo-driven pan-tilt head that spots a horse, locks on and keeps horse and rider in frame, zooming in and out as they move. No radio tags, no QR codes, nothing for the rider to wear: it just watches.

Scroll

The idea

Filming a ride usually means someone standing at the rail with a phone. The cameras that follow you on their own mostly track a radio tag or beacon the rider has to wear. I want one that simply watches: set it on a tripod at the edge of the arena, tap the horse to follow, and it keeps that horse and rider in frame, zooming in for the detail and back out when they head for the far end.

Design goals

No markers
No radio tag, beacon, QR code or phone on the rider. The camera finds them by sight.
Any horse
Works with any horse out of the box, without training it on a particular one.
The right horse
With several horses in the arena, it stays on the one you picked, even when they cross.
Filmed, not twitchy
Slow, steady pans and zooms that keep horse and rider a steady size in the frame, rider included.

The mount

The first sketch: a pan-tilt head on an ordinary camera tripod, with the camera swinging in a U-shaped yoke.

  1. Zoom camera with optical zoom the computer can control, so it can go from the whole arena to one horse and rider without losing detail.
  2. Tilt servo: nods the camera up and down.
  3. Pan servo: turns the whole head left and right.
  4. Vision computer: finds the horses, picks the right one, and drives the servos and the zoom.
  5. Battery, so it can sit at the edge of the arena, away from a socket.
  6. Tripod mount: the standard screw thread every camera tripod has.

How it will track

Every frame goes round the same loop:

Two details make it work without markers:

  • Remembering the right horse. A detector only says "horse here, horse there". To stay on your horse, the tracker follows each one from frame to frame and learns what the chosen one looks like: colour, markings, tack. When two horses cross, or one passes behind another, it picks the right one back up.
  • Linking the rider. People get detected too. A person sitting on the chosen horse belongs to it, so the framing box grows to include them, while someone standing at the rail is ignored.

If the horse disappears behind a jump, a wall or another horse, the camera holds still for a moment, then zooms out and looks for that horse again, rather than settling for whichever one it sees first.

Open questions

  • How fast does it need to pan to keep up with a horse cantering past close to the camera?
  • How well will the vision model cope with dust, a low sun and horses that look alike?
  • Which zoom cameras can a computer control, and how quickly do they zoom?
